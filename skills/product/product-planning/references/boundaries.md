# Boundaries

Load when locking intention, setting boundaries, or naming rabbit
holes. The brief stays **rough** (not a spec), **solved** (main
elements connect), and **bounded** (it says where to stop).

## Kill grab-bags

These are not intentions. Stop and narrow, or refuse them as a plan:

- "Redesign X", "X 2.0", "clean up Y"
- A list of unrelated asks under one name
- A feature that is actually every job a surface could do

Narrow to one story: who was stuck, what was happening, what "done"
looks like. If that story is not validated, say so — this skill does
not discover whether the problem is real.

## Appetite

Ask how much the idea is worth, then fit the solution to that box.
Do not start from a full design and estimate backward.

Write appetite as time, people, or "small enough to ship beside other
work." If no version fits a box the user would pay, that is the
answer — walk away.

Appetite is not a cycle length, a bet, or a deadline promise.

## No-gos

Name what this plan will not do. Pull from the locked non-goals and
from anything the appetite cannot afford. A brief without no-gos is
unbounded.

## Rabbit holes

After the impact scan, list unknowns that could explode scope
(unproven tech, a design problem nobody has solved, a hard product
call left for mid-build). For each, pick one:

| Move | When |
| --- | --- |
| **Settle** | The answer is cheap to decide now; write it in the brief |
| **Cut** | The appetite cannot afford it |
| **Out of bounds** | A real case, but not this plan |

Do not leave a rabbit hole as "we'll see." That is how grab-bags
return. Unverified items that are not scope risks stay under Open
questions.
